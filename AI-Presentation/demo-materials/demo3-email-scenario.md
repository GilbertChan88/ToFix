# SOURCE FOR DEMO 3 — Email + Calendar + Meet scenario
### Use this scenario so the demo feels real and repeatable.
> This gives you a concrete situation to narrate, plus the incoming email you can "reply" to if you'd rather demo a reply than a fresh invite.

---

## The scenario (say this to set it up)
"Imagine I need to organise a 45-minute kick-off for this very AI session with my team of five, next Tuesday afternoon. Watch how far a free account gets me without me typing a single date twice."

## Optional: paste this "incoming email" into Gmail to demo a reply + scheduling
> Send this to yourself before the session so you have a realistic thread to act on. When you reply, Gmail's date detection and "Add to Calendar" are more likely to appear.

```
Subject: Can we get together on the AI session?

Hi,

A few of us would love a short walkthrough before we start using these AI tools.
Could you set up a 45-minute kick-off sometime next week? Tuesday afternoon works
for most of the team. A video link would be great since two people are remote.

Thanks!
Priya
```

## What to point at during the demo (the "prep" value)
- Drafting speed: the invite is written in ~5 seconds.
- Date detection: Gmail recognises "next Tuesday 3:00 PM" and offers **Add to Calendar**.
- Meet link: generated inside the Calendar event with one click.
- Meeting prep: Gemini turns the same context into an agenda + opening questions (Prompt B in the run-sheet).

## Free vs paid — say this plainly
- **Free (personal Google account):** Help me write in Gmail, Add-to-Calendar from email text, create Meet links, and use the Gemini app to draft/summarise/prep.
- **Paid (Google Workspace with Gemini):** auto-suggest meeting times across colleagues' calendars, "Take notes for me" transcription in Meet, and "Ask Gemini" live in a meeting.

## Privacy reminder to voice
"Don't paste anything confidential — client names, personal data — into a free consumer AI when drafting."

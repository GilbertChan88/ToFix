Sleep — why we do it, what actually happens, how to get more of it.

Basics:
- We spend about a third of our lives asleep. Not "switched off" — brain is very active.
- Two big systems control when we feel sleepy: the body clock (circadian rhythm, ~24 hrs, driven by light) and "sleep pressure" (a chemical called adenosine that builds up the longer we're awake). Caffeine blocks adenosine, which is why it wakes us up.

Sleep cycles:
- Sleep runs in cycles of roughly 90 minutes, repeating 4–6 times a night.
- Each cycle has light sleep, deep sleep (slow-wave — physical repair, immune system), and REM (dreaming — memory, emotion, learning).
- Deep sleep is front-loaded early in the night; REM is heavier towards morning. So cutting sleep short cuts REM.

Why it matters:
- Memory: sleep consolidates what we learned during the day. Pulling an all-nighter to study often backfires.
- Mood + focus: poor sleep hits attention, decision-making, emotional control.
- Health: long-term short sleep linked to worse immune function and general wellbeing.

Common myths:
- "I can catch up at the weekend" — you can recover a bit, but it's not a full reset.
- "Older people need much less sleep" — need is similar, but sleep gets lighter/more broken.
- "Alcohol helps me sleep" — it helps you fall asleep but wrecks REM later in the night.

Practical tips that actually help:
- Keep a consistent wake-up time, even weekends — anchors the body clock.
- Get daylight early in the day; dim lights and screens in the last hour before bed.
- Cut caffeine after early afternoon (it lingers 6–8 hrs).
- Cool, dark, quiet room. Bed = sleep only, not work/scrolling.
- If you can't sleep after ~20 min, get up, do something calm, return when sleepy.
